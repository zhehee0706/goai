# Kedai Modern

A poster for a small shop is a shopfront you can scroll past. Kedai Modern treats it the way a good signwriter treats a shophouse board: one thing to see, said with weight and care, in colours that belong to the street. The photo is the shop's own and is never covered by decoration. The design is the frame, the margin and the voice around it. Every poster should look meticulously crafted, as if a typographer and a photo editor spent an afternoon on it, even though the owner spent sixty seconds.

Space comes first. Each poster is built on a page 1080 units wide with a 64-unit margin that nothing crosses except the photo. Type sits on that margin or on the centre line, never somewhere in between. Large quiet areas are deliberate: a bottom band of paper, an inset frame, a wall around a mounted print. Where layers overlap (a sticker over a corner, a price disc over a photo edge), the overlap is a single, intentional gesture placed with painstaking precision, not clutter.

Colour is taken from the photograph. The dominant saturated hue of the shop's own picture becomes the accent, its darkest tone becomes the ground for type, and its palest tone becomes the paper. The result always belongs to the photo, so a matcha poster turns green and a red-bean poster turns red without anyone choosing. Festival work borrows its palette from the season instead (Deepavali plum and marigold, Raya green and gold, New Year red and gold) and keeps the same structure. A fine grain sits over most looks at a few percent, so the work reads as printed matter rather than screen output.

Type does the talking, at three sizes at most: one display line, one supporting line, and micro-type for the shop's signature (name, city, handle) set small and widely tracked. Display faces are chosen per look and used with conviction: a condensed grotesque for hard-working signage, a high-contrast italic serif for evening and premium goods, a warm soft serif for menus and bakeries, a quick hand for snapshots and neon. Prices are typeset, not stickered: tabular figures with a small superior "RM", aligned to the title's baseline. Starbursts, clip art and novelty effects are retired. The mastery shows in the spacing between letters, the alignment of baselines and the confidence of the leading.

The set is a family, not a template. Counter, Noir, Pop, Snapshot, Heritage, Chalkboard, Neon, Colour block, Gallery, Duotone, Risograph, Batik, Contact sheet, Ticket, Menu card and the festive and customer frames each have one idea and execute it with master-level restraint. Each one adapts to every platform's shape (4:5, 3:4, 9:16, 4:3) by moving the same pieces, never by squeezing them. A shop owner should be able to post any of them next to a professional agency's work and have nobody notice which was which.
